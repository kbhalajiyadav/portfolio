## Research motivation

My research asks how thermal, mechanical, and flow histories change the structure of soft and stimuli-responsive materials, and how those changes can be measured reproducibly. A response is scientifically informative only when it can be connected to a defined stimulus, an appropriate structural or mechanical measurement, and analysis that can be repeated. I aim to understand structure–processing–response relationships without treating any one optical or mechanical signal as a complete description of material performance.

## Foundation in experimental measurement

My earlier research at Virginia Commonwealth University examined adhesive interfaces and measurement workflows for flexible and textile-supported systems. As first author of [*Multi-Criteria Selection of Adhesives for Wearable Textiles*](/publication/adhesives-wearable/), I evaluated peel-trace stability, deformation tolerance, and repeatability alongside conventional strength measures to support application-specific adhesive selection. The article also reports collaborative conductive-trace experiments; the [published paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym18121504) provides the formal author-contribution statement.

In the coauthored [dual-layer electrospun-fiber study](/publication/electrospun-fiber-mats/), I contributed mechanical analysis and visualization associated with Figures 4 and 5 and Table 1. The [M.S. thesis](/publication/masters-thesis/) records the degree work, and [versioned peel-trace software](/project/peel-trace-evaluation/) provides a citable implementation of the analysis workflow. These projects emphasized the need to retain measurement context, characterize variability, and distinguish controlled laboratory observations from claims about broad performance.

## Doctoral research direction

My doctoral research direction extends these measurement principles to soft
functional materials. Small-molecule cholesteric mixtures serve as a
**model system** for asking how processing can change internal order and
observable behavior, without treating a model-system result as automatically
transferable to a manufactured device. Three linked questions guide the work.

**How does processing history affect structure?** Heating, cooling, and
shear can change cholesteric textures and rheological response. A mechanistic
question is whether shear reorients an existing helical arrangement, changes
local order, or unwinds the helix. Controlled histories define comparable
states, while scattering may provide structural evidence where contrast and
instrument geometry permit.

**Which measurements distinguish competing interpretations?** Optical
metrology, rheology, and scattering probe different aspects and length scales
of material behavior. I aim to compare their responses under defined
histories, testing which interpretations the combined evidence can support
without assigning unique structural meaning to color, texture, or force alone.

**How can temperature-dependent response be calibrated repeatably?**
Repeated heating and cooling can expose hysteresis, recovery effects, or
changes in an optical response. Calibration, controlled temperature ramps,
and comparison with reference measurements are needed to test whether an
observable response remains quantitatively useful. Automation may improve
repeatability when it preserves stimulus and measurement histories rather
than merely increasing throughput.

## Questions and boundaries

The methods above describe a research direction, not a claim that every
proposed experiment or scattering measurement has been completed. The
longer-term aim is to carry mechanistic insights and validated measurement
methods into responsive materials, passive indicators, and wearable interfaces,
with application-specific testing before any performance claim.

Computational infrared thermography is a **separate exploratory measurement
direction**, not a demonstrated extension of the cholesteric system. In
particular, physiological or metabolic inference from thermal images would
need suitable independent reference measurements and validation; it is not
presented as a current diagnostic capability.

My approach prioritizes controlled comparisons, transparent analysis, and
collaboration where distinct methods answer a shared question. The broader
[Research Program](/research/) connects these priorities to documented
papers, software, and technical projects.
