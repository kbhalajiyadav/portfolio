summary: "A stability-informed, multi-criteria T-peel framework for evaluating adhesive and layered interfaces on soft, stretchable textile substrates."
abstract: >-
  Peel-force magnitude alone can be misleading for soft textile interfaces
  because the measured response includes substrate deformation, peel-arm
  dissipation, failure-path instability, and interfacial separation beyond
  interfacial adhesion alone. Commercial adhesives were evaluated on an 82%
  nylon / 18% spandex knit fabric using a stability-informed T-peel framework
  in which conventional peel strength and IC-Peel apparent fracture energy
  were interpreted alongside displacement at break, Peel Stability Index,
  stick-slip amplitude, and crack-initiation-force repeatability. Although
  E6000 produced the highest peel strength and apparent fracture energy, it
  exceeded the tensile-derived substrate-yield reference and showed the
  largest force oscillations. Fabri-Fuse was selected for its superior
  deformation-stability-repeatability balance, confirmed through functional
  validation. The normalized framework was also extended to fabric-silicone
  interfaces and TPU/PVP/cholesteric-liquid-crystal layered systems across two
  nylon/spandex substrates. The results demonstrate that a stability-informed,
  multi-criteria T-peel framework provides a generalizable characterization
  approach for soft, stretchable textile interfaces by quantifying rather than
  assuming substrate- and formulation-dependent behavior.
